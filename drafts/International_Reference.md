# International Reference: Computer Access Laws in the Six Jurisdictions

Draft for review. General information, not legal advice. Every one of these countries treats getting into someone's computer or account without permission, and deleting their data, as a crime. The United States is covered in Legal_Reference.md. None of this states that anyone broke a law; that is for police, prosecutors or a court to decide.

## 1. United Kingdom: Computer Misuse Act 1990

Section 1 makes unauthorised access to a computer or its data a crime. Section 3 covers unauthorised acts that impair a computer or data, which includes deleting it. Report to Action Fraud (actionfraud.police.uk) in England, Wales and Northern Ireland, or to Police Scotland.

## 2. Canada: Criminal Code, sections 342.1 and 430(1.1)

Section 342.1 covers using a computer or getting computer services without authorization. Section 430(1.1), mischief in relation to computer data, covers destroying, changing or blocking access to data. Report to local police and the Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre.

## 3. Australia: Criminal Code Act 1995 (Commonwealth), Part 10.7

Covers unauthorised access to, modification of, or impairment of computer data, including section 478.1 for restricted data. Each state also has its own computer offences. Report through ReportCyber at cyber.gov.au.

## 4. Switzerland: Swiss Criminal Code, Articles 143bis and 144bis

Article 143bis covers unauthorised access to a data processing system. Article 144bis covers damage to data, including deleting or making it unusable. Report to the cantonal police; the National Cyber Security Centre also takes reports.

## 5. Singapore: Computer Misuse Act 1993

Section 3 covers unauthorised access to computer material. Section 5 covers unauthorised modification, which includes deleting data. Report to the Singapore Police Force.

## 6. Which country applies

Usually the country where you live, the country where the person who got in is, or the country where the data is stored. Gmail data is held by Google, a US company, so US law and US reporting (ic3.gov) are normally the first step for a US resident, with a lawyer deciding whether any other country is involved.
