# Voucher Protection: Today's Gaps and How Our Design Closes Them

Draft for review. Facts come from HUD and the HUD Inspector General; sources are at the end. A true percentage for "the odds" cannot be given until the design is tried, so this compares each known gap with each design feature, and says how to measure the real odds.

## 1. The gaps today

- HUD's FY 2024 report flagged $5.8 billion in rental assistance it could not verify, paid to about 204,000 recipients, including 30,054 people who had died. That is over 11% of nearly $50 billion paid. These are "questionable" payments, not all proven fraud.
- The HUD Inspector General estimated at least $1.43 billion in improper payments in the Housing Choice Voucher program, and found HUD had not produced a required improper-payment estimate in nine years.
- Audits found homes that failed inspection were not re-inspected, and payments that should have been stopped were not.

## 2. Our design, against each gap

| Our design feature | Gap it closes | Closes it? |
|---|---|---|
| Voucher tied to one verified person, re-checked against death and identity records | Payments to dead or ineligible people | Yes, if checks run before every payment |
| Voucher works only for its assigned house | Money used somewhere else | Yes |
| House must pass inspection before any payment, and payment stops the day it fails | Failed homes still getting paid | Yes |
| Every payment recorded: who, which house, when, how much, on a tamper-proof ledger | HUD unable to measure improper payments | Yes: the ledger is the measurement |
| Payment sent on schedule to the house's owner, never as cash | Money not going where it should | Mostly: voucher payments already go to owners today |
| Two people approve each voucher; every approval signed and logged | Staff pressured or blamed; insider fraud | Partly: protects honest staff and shows who approved what |
| The person still chooses the home and is protected from losing it over others' errors | Families hurt by system mistakes | Partly: needs a fast appeal process |

## 3. How to get the real odds

1. Pilot it with one housing authority.
2. Measure its improper payment rate and compare it with today's (more than 11% questionable in FY 2024).
3. Measure how many failed homes get fixed, and how fast.
4. Publish the results. Those numbers are the true odds.
