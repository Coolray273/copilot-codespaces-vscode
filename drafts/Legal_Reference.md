# Legal Reference: Computer Access and Stored Email

Draft for review. General information, not legal advice. None of this states that anyone broke a law; that is for police, prosecutors or a court to decide.

## 1. Maryland: Unauthorized Computer Access (Md. Code, Crim. Law § 7-302)

Makes it a crime to intentionally access a computer, network or database without authorization, or to go beyond the access you were given. Penalties increase with intent and the amount of damage.

## 2. Federal: Computer Fraud and Abuse Act (18 U.S.C. § 1030)

Federal counterpart to § 7-302. Covers accessing a computer without authorization or exceeding authorized access. Van Buren v. United States, 593 U.S. 374 (2021), read "exceeds authorized access" narrowly: it applies when someone enters parts of a system that are off-limits to them, not when they misuse access they legitimately have.

## 3. Federal: Stored Communications Act (18 U.S.C. § 2701)

Covers getting into stored electronic communications, such as email held by Gmail, without authorization, and obtaining, changing or blocking access to them. This is the law most likely to apply if someone else got into a Gmail account and deleted messages.

## 4. Federal: Wiretap Act (18 U.S.C. § 2511)

Covers intercepting communications while they are being transmitted. It usually does not cover messages that were already stored and later deleted; those fall under the Stored Communications Act.

## 5. Federal: CAN-SPAM Act (15 U.S.C. § 7701 and following)

Covers commercial marketing email: deceptive headers and subject lines, and ignoring unsubscribe requests. It probably does not apply to deleted personal emails or a blocked account.

## 6. What to do with this

1. Preserve evidence: screenshots of Google security activity, a Google Takeout export, and SHA-256 hashes of saved files with the dates they were made.
2. Report suspected unauthorized access to the FBI at ic3.gov and to local police for a case number.
3. Have a lawyer review the facts before anyone sends a legal notice. The Maryland State Bar Association can give a referral; Maryland Volunteer Lawyers Service offers free help.
